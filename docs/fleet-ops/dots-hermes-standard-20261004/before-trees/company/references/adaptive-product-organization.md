# Adaptive product organization — owner target

## Confirmed intent

The owner wants a profit-generating AI organization managing multiple products, not merely a fixed fleet assisting one project. It may start by proving a complete outcome loop on the project nearest to revenue, but must retain the ability to expand across the portfolio. Product quality and profit are the objectives; agent count and minimal owner time are not objectives by themselves.

The owner is willing to contribute as much time as necessary. Use that availability for expertise, capabilities, meaningful product feedback and material decisions, not as a reason to make the owner a routine scheduler or approval relay. Existing serious-risk boundaries, budget limits and evidence gates remain unchanged.

## Owner-specific implementation scope

Build for the owner's existing Hermes desktop installation, approved resources and profit-oriented products; portability and a distributable company framework are not current deliverables. Prefer native app capabilities and the smallest tested fixes over new architecture layers. Keep direct company ownership of repair until autonomous execution is accepted. Recovery must distinguish service/worker failure from loss of the entire Hermes process or host: use supported existing host supervision for the latter, never promise that an inactive agent can repair itself. Automatic recovery is bounded, identity-checked, reversible and verified by readback; unknown side effects are reconciled before retry. Preserve the owner's restrictions on updates, models/pools, paid capabilities and destructive actions. Escalate only the precise unresolved boundary, not routine scheduling.

## Organizational design

- Keep a small shared executive/control layer for portfolio allocation, independent verification, financial controls and shared infrastructure.
- Give each active product one accountable lead (role assignment first; a dedicated persistent profile only when justified), metric, budget and explicit operating scope.
- Allow product-specific support agents with scoped tools and a product knowledge base, content-channel teams, and other operational roles when recurring demand, required isolation or economics justify them.
- Prefer a script, existing skill or temporary task for deterministic or one-off work. Create a durable agent for recurring responsibility, distinct state/knowledge, clear access boundaries or service-level requirements. Do not duplicate the full shared fleet for every product by default.
- Compare build versus adopt for external solutions, including content-production pipelines. Evaluate license, security, maintainability, integration effort, quality and total operating cost before adoption; first pilot narrowly and reversibly.
- Define an agent lifecycle: purpose and owner → workload/economic justification → scoped tools and knowledge → independent evaluation → bounded pilot → monitoring → expand, revise or retire. Creating a profile is not proof that the service works.
- Product-specific RAG must have approved sources, freshness/versioning, product/tenant separation, access controls, answer evaluation and an uncertainty/escalation path. A vector index alone is not verified support competence.
- Judge content operations by qualified demand, retained audience and conversion under quality/reputation constraints, not posting volume. Judge support by correct resolution, reopen rate, escalation quality and cost per resolved case, not messages sent.

## Sequencing

When the owner requests direct repair because autonomous chains are unreliable, keep implementation under the active company operator rather than creating another repair chain. A request to fully finish the company and its contour is an end-to-end acceptance scope, not a series of unrelated maintenance wins: map metric/capital input, company decision, executable contract, delivery, independent verification, outcome observation and next-action consumption to actual producers and consumers. Preserve a single gap-to-acceptance ledger in the repair dossier; take local repairs as increments only, and do not end the overall job because one watcher or unit suite is green. Separate temporary diagnostics from the durable native operating loop; do not create a second dispatcher/tracker to compensate for a missing consumer. Preserve evidence, stop the explicitly cancelled workers (a blocked task is not proof its process exited), and use isolated source fixes plus reproducible regression tests. Do not count a restored health endpoint, a new task, or local green tests as full readiness. Preserve independent release verification; do not turn direct ownership into self-approval or weaken gates. Resume autonomous execution only against demonstrated lifecycle and outcome acceptance.

Repair current safety/liveness defects without globally weakening gates. Demonstrate one real product outcome loop, then repeat the operating model on another product with actual evidence of cross-product isolation, resource allocation and reduced marginal setup cost. Do not interpret a temporary pause on unnecessary agent expansion as a permanent fixed-roster policy.

## Investment discipline for evolution plans

Do not make generic telemetry, evidence projections, model benchmarks or fleet-wide skill cleanup prerequisites for permitted customer discovery. Start with one reachable buyer, honest offer, allowed channel, delivery acceptance, resource cap and observation deadline in the existing root card; preserve every applicable sale/release/payment gate. A concierge label never bypasses a product readiness hold.

Fund internal improvements when a current commercial workflow or material safety risk needs them. Prefer native handoffs and tools before new packets/wrappers; measure benchmark and maintenance cost as well as any model savings. Insufficient signal should trigger a bounded channel/source test, not endless discovery or a claim that demand is absent. A negative experiment can protect capital but is not revenue.
